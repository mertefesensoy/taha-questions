Recurring techniques Four ideas do most of the work across these nineteen problems. Counting degrees settles 9, 10 and 11. Counting components against edges — the Edge Addition Lemma and its consequence that a forest on \(n\) vertices with \(c\) components has \(n-c\) edges — settles 12, 14, 17, 18, 19, 22 and the starred problem, and supplies the cycle used in 23. Bounding component sizes from below by degrees settles 13 and 20. And double counting a set of incidences — the handshake lemma in disguise — settles 26. The remaining problems (15, 16, 21, 24, 25) are settled by an explicit construction together with an argument that no other kind exists.
