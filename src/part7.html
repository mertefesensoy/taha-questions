PROBLEM 7

Minimum degree \(n/2\) forces connectivity

two proofs; sharpness
Problem

Let \(G\) be a simple graph and assume that \(d(v)\ge \frac{n}{2}\) holds for all of its vertices. Prove that \(G\) is connected.

Here \(n=|V(G)|\) is the number of vertices, so the hypothesis is \(\delta(G)\ge n/2\). We give two proofs. The first is the shortest; the second costs one extra line and yields the stronger conclusion \(\operatorname{diam}(G)\le2\) — any two vertices are joined by a path of length at most \(2\).

Theorem 7.1 Let \(G\) be a simple graph on \(n\ge1\) vertices with \(d(v)\ge n/2\) for every \(v\in V(G)\). Then \(G\) is connected, and moreover \(\operatorname{diam}(G)\le 2\).

Proof 1 — via the size of a smallest component

Proof. Suppose, for contradiction, that \(G\) is not connected. Then \(G\) has at least two components; let \(H_1\) and \(H_2\) be two distinct ones and let \(H\) be the smaller of the two, say with \(h=|V(H)|\) vertices. Distinct components are vertex‑disjoint subsets of \(V(G)\), so \[ 2h \;\le\; |V(H_1)|+|V(H_2)| \;\le\; n , \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad h \;\le\; \frac{n}{2}. \] Pick any vertex \(v\in V(H)\). By Lemma D all neighbours of \(v\) lie in \(H\), and \(v\) is not its own neighbour (\(G\) is simple, so there are no loops); hence \[ d(v) \;=\; d_H(v) \;\le\; |V(H)|-1 \;=\; h-1 \;\le\; \frac{n}{2}-1 \; \lt \; \frac{n}{2} . \] This contradicts the hypothesis \(d(v)\ge n/2\). Therefore \(G\) is connected. □

Proof 2 — via a common neighbour

Proof. Let \(u\ne v\) be any two vertices of \(G\); we exhibit a \(u\)–\(v\) path of length at most \(2\).

Case 1: \(uv\in E(G)\). Then \(uv\) is itself a \(u\)–\(v\) path of length \(1\).

Case 2: \(uv\notin E(G)\). We claim \(N(u)\cap N(v)\neq\emptyset\). First locate the two neighbourhoods. Since \(G\) is simple there are no loops, so \(u\notin N(u)\) and \(v\notin N(v)\); and since \(uv\notin E(G)\) we also have \(v\notin N(u)\) and \(u\notin N(v)\). Hence both \(N(u)\) and \(N(v)\) are contained in \(V(G)\setminus\{u,v\}\), and therefore \[ \bigl|N(u)\cup N(v)\bigr| \;\le\; n-2 . \] By inclusion–exclusion and the degree hypothesis, \[ \bigl|N(u)\cap N(v)\bigr| \;=\; |N(u)|+|N(v)| - \bigl|N(u)\cup N(v)\bigr| \;\ge\; \frac{n}{2}+\frac{n}{2}-(n-2) \;=\; 2 \; \gt \; 0 . \] So there exists (in fact there exist at least two) \(w\in N(u)\cap N(v)\), and \(u\,w\,v\) is a \(u\)–\(v\) path of length \(2\) (it is a path because \(w\notin\{u,v\}\)).

In both cases \(\operatorname{dist}(u,v)\le 2 \lt \infty\). As \(u,v\) were arbitrary, every pair of vertices is joined by a path, i.e. \(G\) is connected, and \(\operatorname{diam}(G)\le2\).

□
Answer

\(G\) is connected. If it were not, its smallest component \(H\) would satisfy \(|V(H)|\le n/2\), and any vertex \(v\) of \(H\) would have all its neighbours inside \(H\), giving \(d(v)\le |V(H)|-1\le n/2-1 \lt n/2\) — contradicting the hypothesis. The second proof shows more: for non‑adjacent \(u,v\) the sets \(N(u),N(v)\) both live in the \((n-2)\)-element set \(V\setminus\{u,v\}\) while \(|N(u)|+|N(v)|\ge n\), so they must meet; any common neighbour \(w\) gives a path \(u\,w\,v\). Hence in fact \(\operatorname{diam}(G)\le 2\).

The hypothesis is sharp

The constant \(n/2\) cannot be lowered. For even \(n\), let

\[ G_0 \;=\; K_{n/2} \ \dot\cup\ K_{n/2} \]

be the disjoint union of two complete graphs of \(n/2\) vertices each. Every vertex of \(G_0\) is adjacent to all the other \(n/2-1\) vertices of its own clique and to nothing else, so

\[ d(v) \;=\; \frac{n}{2}-1 \qquad \text{for every } v, \]

which is only \(1\) less than required — and \(G_0\) is disconnected. So the implication genuinely fails as soon as the bound is weakened by one.

The exact threshold Degrees are integers, so for odd \(n\) the hypothesis \(d(v)\ge n/2\) really says \(d(v)\ge\lceil n/2\rceil =\frac{n+1}{2}\), which is more than necessary. Repeating Proof 1 for odd \(n\): a smallest component satisfies \(2h\le n\), hence \(h\le\frac{n-1}{2}\) and \(d(v)\le\frac{n-3}{2}\); so already \(\delta(G)\ge\frac{n-1}{2}=\lfloor n/2\rfloor\) forces connectivity. Combining the two parities, the sharp statement is: \[ \delta(G)\ \ge\ \Bigl\lfloor \tfrac{n}{2} \Bigr\rfloor \ \Longrightarrow\ G \text{ is connected,} \] and this is best possible for every \(n\): the graph \(K_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\ \dot\cup\ K_{\lceil n/2\rceil}\) has \(\delta=\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1\) and is disconnected.
Context: Dirac's theorem Theorem 7.1 is the easy shadow of a much deeper result with the same hypothesis. Dirac's theorem (1952) states that if \(n\ge3\) and \(\delta(G)\ge n/2\) then \(G\) is Hamiltonian: it contains a cycle through all \(n\) vertices. A Hamiltonian graph is obviously connected, so Dirac's theorem implies Problem 7 — but "connected" is the part one can prove in three lines, as above. It is instructive that the same threshold \(n/2\) is sharp for both statements, and for the same reason: two disjoint cliques.
